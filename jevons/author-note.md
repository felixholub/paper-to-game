# Jevons's Noon: author note

**Look first at** the ruler: slide it and read both rows, how big the fire gets and how long the coal lasts. Then the 1885 engine: fit it "to make the coal last", set a stand, and watch the solid line climb over your dotted one. Play to the end: the sheet runs on to 2065 so a fast fire is seen to fall, and the verdict reads height and length. Then *The curve, continued*: the last screen has no words, by design (your Given).

**The config block** is at the top of `jevons/mechanism.js`. Every number is tagged with where it comes from: the frontispiece, the preface, ch. I, ch. VII, or "our choice". The words the game says are in `jevons/words.js`, and the page is built by `python3 jevons/build.py`.

**Least sure about:** the sizes the toy had to invent: the pull of cheap power (2.5), the engine rebound (0.5), the share of the jobless who emigrate, and America's 6 per cent. `calibration.js` shows that the directions hold when each is moved both ways, but the verdict's numbers (the noon year, the millions over the sea) are the toy's. Also: the real series on the last screen is checked from 1900 on, but 1865–1899 is recalled from Mitchell, and Hull's 83,544 is from ch. II, which was not in the PDF. The DESNZ spreadsheet and Econlib were blocked from the build machine, so both are worth a glance.

The game has had the builder's review only (screenshots and a scripted browser session); no stranger has played it yet.
