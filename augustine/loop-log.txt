THE LAST PAGE
  YOU KEPT TIME WITH YOUR MIND, UNTIL A WHEEL RAN AWAY WITH YOU.
  1. You measured seven times with no clock. Going by the voice in your memory, you came within about a tenth of it.
  1. When the wheel raced, you let go at about half the voice, just where counting its turns would put you.
  1. When the sun stood still, you let go all the same: time went on.
  1. Your long syllables came out nearly double your short ones, and you held the first voice to about nine tenths of its length, minutes after it stopped.
  2. Augustine: a wheel and the sun are motions, and we measure motions by time, so they are not time. A voice can’t be measured while it sounds, and it is gone once it stops. What you measured each time was what it left in you.
  3. "It is in thee, my mind, that I measure times." For Augustine, time is a stretching of the mind (his word is distentio), between what it expects and what it remembers.
  psalm: This evening too: before you began, all seven rounds lay ahead of you, in expectation. Now they are all in memory. Augustine says it is the same with a psalm said by heart, with every syllable of it, and with each longer thing it is part of.
  before: And before there was anything at all? Augustine refuses the old joke that God was “preparing hell for pryers into mysteries”. There was no before: time began with the things that were made. "There was no “then,” when there was no time."
