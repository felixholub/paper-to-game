You settled six of the seven that could be settled.
  You settled all four of the clashes that began by accident, for the fewest pages they could take.
  Of the five started on purpose, the crowd took the challenger's side in three, and you settled two of those; the crowd took three of your pages.
  The two started to pull a body down could not be settled at any price, and the two pages you gave them were lost.
  Kreuder-Sonnen, Schmotz and Zürn studied 77 real clashes between international rulebooks, 1970 to 2018. Of the 35 that began by accident, 33 were settled together, and most knit the two bodies closer. Of the 42 started on purpose, 16 were fought to the end, and all but one tore the system apart; 26 were settled after all, most of them for good.
  So the two old camps were both right, about different clashes. Overlapping rulebooks pull the world together when they collide by accident, and apart when someone collides them on purpose, unless whoever picked the fight has the world on their side.
