# Curie's Quartz — note for the author

1. Look first at `screenshot-a-bench-pitchblende-chase.png` (the thumb past the uranium mark, the spot still running, the "!?"), `screenshot-a-bench-pitchblende-balanced.png`, `screenshot-a-bench-copy-balanced.png` (the green crystal made from pure chemicals), `screenshot-a-page-full.png` (the album page the player draws) and `screenshot-a-end-top.png` (the verdict). Then play it: the balance is the game, and only a thumb can judge it.
2. Everything tunable is in the `CONFIG` block at the top of `mechanism.js`, the only copy of the mechanism: her currents with the table row each came from; the uranium shares (ours); white phosphorus's number (ours); the electrometer's feel (`G`, `TAU`, `BAND`, `HOLD_S`, `JITTER`); the nine panels; the cut rows of the table, kept for you. Edit, run `node calibration.js` (67 checks against the table, her sentences on p. 1102, four kinds of thumb, robustness, and the shelf in numbers), then `python3 build.py`, which refuses to finish if the embedded mechanism differs from the file.
3. What we were least sure about: (a) the instrument is ours: she let a weight down by hand and timed it (thesis, 1903); the game lets the player set a steady push, which measures the same thing but is not how it felt in her hands; (b) the uranium shares on the yellow bars are ours, and two rows of her table that sit awkwardly with "in general" (black oxide above the metal, uranic acid well below) are left off the shelf, declared on About; (c) white phosphorus has no number in the note; we gave it a third more than uranium; (d) the verdict's last line (polonium in July, radium in December 1898) is after the note, and is marked so.

This game has had the builder's review only: screenshots and scripted browser sessions at 380 px. It has not been played by real strangers, and the five-to-six-minute length is an estimate.
