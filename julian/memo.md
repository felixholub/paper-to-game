# Julian's Window — design memo

## 0. Given

Mine (Steffen's signature and counter). Ideas given, and binding: *The look is manga, shōjo not shōnen: ink, screentone, stars and flowers, wide-eyed wonder, quiet panels. One accent colour at most. The blood is drawn, never red. The cell is one room with one window.*

Where this overrode precedent: the register family. The last three games are Drawn (Goodall's notebook), Worldly (Weil's vase) and Retro (Foot's silent film); the rule says not another Drawn one, and manga is Drawn. The Given wins, and everything inside the family differs from Goodall (white page and ink and tone where she had cream and graphite; comic lettering where she had a typewriter; panels where she had a plate). "The cell" fixes the play screen: the text's sickroom ("it was all dark about me in the chamber", ch. iii) is drawn as a cell, one room, one window, and the window is where the night is told.

## 1. Paper

Julian of Norwich, *Revelations of Divine Love* (the Long Text, written c. 1393 from the shewings of 8 May 1373), translated by Grace Warrack (1901), chapters i–xxxii, as issued by the Christian Classics Ethereal Library (ccel.org/ccel/julian/revelations). The PDF in `inbox/` is those 32 chapters with Warrack's notes; chapters xxxiii–lxxxvi (the lord and the servant, the motherhood of God, the last shewings) were not available and nothing from them is used. No data, no code: a text. Online at ccel.org.

## 2. What the paper hands over

An **argument**, delivered as a report of sights. Primary: a model of the soul's weather. Within the Seventh Shewing (ch. xv) Julian is "fulfilled with the everlasting sureness", then "turned and left to myself in heaviness", then comforted, "and now that one, and now that other, divers times — I suppose about twenty times"; she tests the obvious cause and rules it out ("in this time I sinned not wherefore I should be left to myself"; "also I deserved not to have this blessed feeling"); she concludes that the swing is given freely and that "God willeth that we know that He keepeth us even alike secure in woe and in weal", and draws the practical rule: "it is not God's will that we follow the feelings of pain in sorrow and mourning for them, but that we suddenly pass over." Two quantities, one swinging and visible, one constant and not. Secondary: the economy of looking. All sixteen shewings are *sights* ("by bodily sight, and by word formed in mine understanding, and by spiritual sight", ch. ix), and where she looks is her one act all night: she "set my eyes on the face of the Crucifix"; "all that was away from the Cross was of horror to me" (ch. iii); "I would have looked up from the Cross, but I durst not" and, offered "Look up to Heaven", answered "Nay; I may not" (ch. xix); and what comes when she looks is not hers to command ("If God will shew thee more, He shall be thy light", ch. x; "I weened to have seen her in bodily presence, but I saw her not so", ch. xxv; "I thought to have seen the body all dead; but I saw Him not so", ch. xxi). Formalised as a toy, with the choices the text did not make listed in §15 and §16.

## 3. The discovery, and its kind

**A counterintuitive result.** What everyone believes, in 1373 and now: when the dark comes over you it is about you; you did something, or you have been left. Julian, inside the dark about twenty times in a day, checks and finds otherwise: it followed nothing she did ("sin is not always the cause"), it lifted for nothing she did ("freely our Lord giveth when He will"), and the thing that kept her did not move the whole time. The feeling is weather; the keeping is constant; the right act is not to read the weather as a verdict. The rest of the text's famous lines (the hazelnut that lasts because it is loved, "seeking is as good as beholding", "Where is now any point of thy pain?", "sin is behovable, but all shall be well") are the same discovery seen from other sides, and they land on the verdict screen.

Also a **mechanism made intelligible**: the change from woe to weal is instantaneous ("betwixt that one and that other shall be no time", ch. xxi) and comes to the one who stays at the cross "unto the last point".

## 4. The core narrative

A young woman lies in a bed in a small stone room with one window, so ill that she is sure she will die before morning. The priest has hung a little cross on the wall in front of her face. When she looks at it she sees things nobody else in the room can see: blood running like rain off a roof, a face going grey and dry, then the same face suddenly glad. All night a heaviness comes over the room and goes again, like weather, and every time it comes she thinks: what did I do? She tries looking at the window instead, and looking inside herself, and asking. By morning she has found out that the heaviness was never caused by anything she did, that the thing keeping her warm was the same the whole night, and she has heard the sentence she will spend twenty years writing down: all shall be well.

## 5. Who lives in the paper

- **Julian, "a simple creature unlettered"**, thirty and a half, dying. Controls: where she looks (the crucifix, up, away, within); what she asks ("What may this be?" "What is sin?" "Is any pain like this?" "How might all be well?"); what she says ("Benedicite Domine!", "Nay; I may not", "Yea, good Lord, I thank Thee"); whether she follows the feeling ("Lord, save me: I perish!") or passes over. She is also the text's experimenter: she asked for the sickness as a trial (ch. ii), doubted the second shewing (ch. x), counted the swings, checked the cause (ch. xv), "beheld with great diligence to learn how often He would die" (ch. xxii).
- **The curate**, who sets the cross before her face: "look thereupon and comfort thee therewith." Controls one thing, once.
- **Them that were about her**, who laugh when she laughs (ch. xiii) and are told "It is to-day Doomsday with me" (ch. viii). Control nothing; see nothing.
- **The Lord**, who shows, speaks "without voice and opening of lips", asks ("Art thou well pleased?" "Wilt thou see her?"), and changes cheer. Controls the weather and the sights; is not a player.
- **The Fiend**, overcome, scorned, laughed at; "he hath as much sorrow when God giveth him leave to work, as when he worketh not" (ch. xiii): an agent whose payoff is flat in his own action.
- **The hazelnut**, all that is made, which "might suddenly have fallen to naught for littleness" and lasts because it is loved (ch. v). No choice.
- **The blood**, which cannot be counted (ch. vii). No choice.
- **The even-Christian**, for whom she writes, told to "leave the beholding of a poor creature" and look past her (ch. viii). The reader; not in the room.

**What this game does differently.**

- **Position: the witness as experimenter.** Not an animal in its body, not a soul riding a warrior's arm, not the player in their own shoes: the player is the text's own witness, whose eyes are the instrument and whose night is a test of what brings the dark. *I looked at the cross through the heaviness, then tried the window, then looked within for the sin that had brought it, and found none.*
- **Format: discovery.** The last three are two challenges and a staircase of dilemmas. This is a hidden system probed: the weather turns on its own clock, the player acts and watches what it follows, and the record is read at the end. No par, no score, no rival.
- **Device: the page's weather and the one thing that does not change.** Screentone thickening and stars thinning over a room drawn once; a blanket in the one colour that is the same in every panel; the sky in the window telling the hour; a strip of sixteen small panels along the foot of the page, the night so far. No stem, no scales, no staircase.
- **Chassis: shōjo manga** (Drawn, by the Given; the memo notes above that this repeats Goodall's family). A white page after a black vase and a nitrate screen; ink and dot-tone where Goodall had graphite; Delius comic lettering and an Italiana title where she had a typewriter; panel borders and speech balloons where she had paper slips. Palette, type and buttons share nothing with the three.

## 7. The aim and the verdict

**The aim**, as the player reads it, on its own line: *See the night through. Each time, choose where to look — the cross, the window, or within — and find out what brings the heaviness.*

**The yardstick**, in the aim's units: the sky in the window (how much of the night is done) and the strip of panels at the foot of the page (each beat so far: its weather, and where you looked). Both describe; neither judges.

**The verdict** leads with three lines, in words:

1. *What you did.* "The heaviness lay on the room seven times. In it you stayed at the cross four times, went to the window twice, and once looked within for the sin that had brought it, and found none." Then what was seen and what was missed: "You saw the garland, the drops, the drying, the cheer change, the side. You were at the window when the face was shown."
2. *What Julian says.* "It followed nothing you did and lifted for nothing you did; she checked the same: 'I sinned not wherefore I should be left to myself; also I deserved not to have this blessed feeling.' The blanket never changed: 'He keepeth us even alike secure in woe and in weal.' And: 'it is not God's will that we follow the feelings of pain in sorrow and mourning for them, but that we suddenly pass over.'"
3. *What that means.* "The dark that comes over you is not a message about you. Between the dark and the light there is no time. What you saw, you saw by staying; and seeking was as good as beholding."

Then her sentence, alone on the page: *All shall be well, and all shall be well, and all manner of thing shall be well.* No tally.

## 8. Three concepts

**A. Julian's Window** — *experimenter, discovery.* The player is Julian in the bed. Sixteen beats, one night. Each beat the room is drawn with its weather on it (weal: stars and petals; woe: heavy tone, scribbles at the edges), and the player chooses where she looks: **the cross** (the next sight of the Passion, sweet in weal and dreadful in woe), **the window** (the sky and the hour, nothing else; relief; "nothing betwixt the Cross and Heaven that might have harmed me"), or **within** (a question, answered: the hazelnut; what is sin; is any pain like this; what brought this heaviness; how might all be well; and one question that gets a wall). The weather turns on its own hidden clock, about half the beats. Deliberation: in the dark, stay with a sight that is getting worse, or go to the window and miss what comes; and, each time the dark comes, what it followed. Discovery moment: the dark comes for the first time at beat two or three, right after something the player did; it lifts after something else; by the fourth time the pattern is not there, and the blanket has not moved once. The change of cheer ("Where is now any point of thy pain?") comes only to a player at the cross when the weather turns, with no time between. Fun: anticipation (what will the window show this hour; what comes next at the cross), discovery (the weather is not mine; the blanket), agency (what she sees is what she looked at), mastery (the second half of the night, played knowing). Second playthrough: a different weather schedule, and the other strategy: the window every dark beat, or within every beat, to see the other sights and the same record.

**B. The Fiend at the Window** — *agent, strategic; the reckless one.* The player is the Fiend outside the one window, working on the girl in the bed through the night: send heaviness, whisper that it is her sin, fill the corners with horror, urge her to count the drops, urge her to pry into who is damned. Each works on the surface (she darkens, she asks) and then "turneth us to joy and him to shame": she laughs, the people laugh, the sight grows. The Fiend's own sorrow meter is flat whatever he does ("as much sorrow when God giveth him leave to work, as when he worketh not"); doing nothing reads the same. Deliberation: which lever, then the realisation that no lever moves the meter, then the test of idleness. Discovery: a player whose payoff is constant in his own action, named at the moment it happens ("I see game, scorn, and earnest"). Fun: discovery, a laugh. Thin after minute three; second playthrough offers nothing new. Walkthrough in §9.

**C. The Inward Part** — *subject, challenge.* The player is the inward part of Julian (ch. xix: "the inward part is master and sovereign to the outward"); the outward part, the body in the bed, proposes all night ("Look up to Heaven"; "Lord, save me: I perish"; "If I had wist what pain it had been, loth me had been to have prayed it") and the player assents or overrides. Deliberation: whether to let the body have its way this once. Discovery: "repenting and willing choice be two contraries which I felt both in one at that time"; the body's repenting carries no blame. Fun: agency, anticipation. This is Weil's position (a soul inside a body, holding an arm back), the text gives the overrides no consequence to score, and the choice collapses to "override every time".

## 10. The pick, and why

**A, Julian's Window.** It gives the stranger the best five minutes with real deliberation in them: in the dark, stay with a sight that is getting worse or go to the window, not knowing when the weather will turn; and, each time the dark comes, the question of what it followed, which the stranger asks on their own because the first two times it follows something. The discovery lands inside the play: the pattern is not there by the fourth dark, and the blanket has not moved, and the change of cheer rewards the one who stayed, with no time between. The sentence afterwards has no "guess": *I looked at the cross through the heaviness, tried the window once, looked within for the sin that brought it and found none, and the blanket was the same all night.* Originality: no game in the table has made the player's own feeling the noise and the keeping the signal, nor built a night out of where a witness looks.

**Author test.** Julian would recognise every panel: the cross set before her face, the horror away from it, the three likenesses of the drops, the face half covered, "Look up to Heaven" and "Nay; I may not", the twenty swings and her own control check, "Where is now any point of thy pain?", the hazelnut, the wall before the hid part, and the sentence. She wrote it so that her even-Christians "might see and know the same that I saw"; a game that lets a stranger run her own check on the dark is that. Steffen would enjoy a model in which the visible state carries no information about the hidden one and the payoff is a step function the text refuses to grade ("for one day's service... all these three degrees of bliss", ch. xiv): there is nothing to maximise, which is why there is no score.

**Runner-up: B, the Fiend.** Switch if the author wants a ninety-second joke for a lecture on dominated strategies: a player whose payoff is constant in his own action, and a girl who laughs.

**What the reckless concept would have given.** The laugh, and the Fiend as the only agent in the text whose game theory is written out. Kept from it: the laugh (ch. xiii is a panel at the cross: she laughs, the people laugh), and the scribbles at the edges of the dark pages, which are his. Given up: playing him.

## 11. The shape

One night, sixteen beats (the text's sixteen shewings, as a count; the sights drawn are about ten of them). Screens: cover (title, the aim, Turn the page, How to play, About) → page one (the three days in four small panels, the cross set, the room explained) → sixteen play pages → the record (the strip whole) → the verdict → Another night. Presets are the text's own regimes, ordered for the player: the **sights at the cross** in the text's order (the garland bleeding, ch. iv; the drops' three likenesses, ch. vii; the face half covered, vanishing, ch. x; the scourging, ch. xii; the laugh, ch. xiii; the drying, ch. xvi–xvii; "Look up to Heaven", ch. xix, offered from the window at the deepest dark; the change of cheer, ch. xxi, only at the cross when the weather turns; "Art thou well pleased?", ch. xxii; the side, ch. xxiv; "Wilt thou see her?", ch. xxv; "It is I", ch. xxvi); the **questions within** in the text's order (the hazelnut, ch. v; what is sin, ch. xi; is any pain like this, ch. xvii; what brought this heaviness, ch. xv; why was sin not hindered, ch. xxvii; how might all be well, ch. xxix; the hid part, ch. xxx and xxxii); the **window** by the hour (black, stars, grey, light). The weather's first turn is forced by beat three so the discovery arrives early. No score.

## 12. Controls, mechanism, feedback, moment

- **Control:** one, where she looks, three live options each beat: the cross, the window, within. Tap a place in the drawing (or its labelled panel under the drawing): her eyes turn to it and a line says what looking there is; tap *Look* to commit. Every beat starts with her eyes on nothing.
- **Mechanism:** the weather `w ∈ {weal, woe}` turns on a seeded hidden schedule, independent of the player by construction; the keeping is constant; each place has a queue of sights that advances when looked at; a sight at the cross reads sweet in weal and dreadful in woe (ch. vii: "horrifying and dreadful, sweet and lovely"); the change of cheer fires when the player is at the cross on a woe→weal turn; the window's proffer fires in the deepest dark; the hid question returns the wall. The record keeps, per beat, the weather, the place, and what came.
- **Feedback:** the page. Tone and stars over the room (the weather); the sky in the window (the hour); her eyes (where she looks, and how wide); the sight panel, large; a balloon for His words, a thought bubble for hers, a caption box for the game's voice; the strip along the foot, one small panel per beat; the blanket, rose, unchanged.
- **Moment:** the fourth dark, which follows nothing in particular, with the blanket the same; and the first change of cheer, which comes to a player who stayed, with no time between.

## 13. The look

**Register: shōjo manga** (Drawn family), given. The paper's own subject supplies it anyway: a young woman in a bed, wide-eyed, seeing things, in a text that is nothing but what she saw, told in panels ("the First is...", "the Second is..."). Shōjo, not shōnen: no speed lines, no shouting; stars, petals, tone, quiet.

- **Palette (named):** *page* `#FBFAF5` (white, everything sits on it); *ink* `#17150F` (every line, the lettering, the tone dots, the scribbles); *tone* is the ink at 10–55 % as dot screens, never a grey fill; *blanket rose* `#E7A2B6`, the one accent, on the blanket and nowhere else. The blood is ink: pellets, scales, drops. No red anywhere.
- **Type:** Delius (Natalia Raices, SIL OFL), a comic lettering face, for every balloon, caption, button and label; Italiana (Santiago Orozco, SIL OFL), a thin high-contrast serif, for the title and the hour marks only. Both embedded as data.
- **Buttons:** panels. A thin ink border, square corners, white inside, the label in Delius; the primary action (*Look*, *Turn the page*) is a black panel with white lettering; a place that cannot be looked at this beat is drawn with its border broken. Her choices are not buttons but the drawing itself.
- **The one visual rule:** the room is drawn once and never redrawn; only what lies over it changes (tone, stars, petals, scribbles, the sky in the window, her eyes), and the blanket is the one thing in colour and is the same in every panel of the night.
- **Play screen at 380 px:** the aim (one line) with ? at the right; the page, 348 × 300: the room in ink, bed at the left with the blanket in rose and her head on the pillow, eyes large; the cross on the wall at the foot of the bed; the window at the right with the sky in it (the hour); the weather over everything (answers "how does it feel in here, and what hour is it?"); under the page, three small labelled panels (the cross / the window / within) that mirror the three places (answers "what can I do?"), and the *Look* panel; after the look, the sight panel replaces the three (the thing she sees, drawn large: answers "what did looking there bring?"), His balloon or her thought, the caption in the game's voice (what you did, what came, what the weather did, what that means), *Turn the page*; the strip along the foot, sixteen small frames filling left to right, each with its weather and a mark for the place (answers "what has the night been so far, and what did the dark follow?").
- **Record page:** the strip at full width, two rows of eight, the blanket drawn once as a rose line under all sixteen; then the three verdict lines; her sentence; *Another night*, How to play, About.

## 15. Divergences

- **Clock.** The sixteen shewings came over a day and the following night; the swing of about twenty is one shewing's. Here: one night of sixteen beats, and the weather turns about half of them (eight in expectation, between five and eleven). The twenty is in `CONFIG` with its chapter; the compression is declared.
- **The weather as a process.** The text says the swing came "freely", "when He will"; the game makes that a seeded random schedule, independent of the player by construction, with the first turn forced by beat three. Ours.
- **The window.** The text has no window in these chapters. The Given's window carries "Look up to Heaven" (ch. xix: safe, nothing shown) and the hour (ours). The horror "away from the Cross" (ch. iii) is at the room's edges, as scribbles, in woe only.
- **The blanket.** "He is our clothing that for love wrappeth us, claspeth us, and all encloseth us" (ch. v) drawn as the blanket, in the one colour, constant. The drawing is ours; the constancy is ch. xv.
- **Sights by weather.** Ch. vii says one sight was "horrifying and dreadful, sweet and lovely" at once; the game shows a sight dreadful in woe and sweet in weal. The split is ours.
- **The questions.** In the text they arise when He shows; here the player chooses when to look within, and the queue is in the text's order.
- **The change of cheer** is tied to being at the cross when the weather turns (ch. xxi: "willingly abiding in the same Cross... unto the last point, suddenly He shall change His Cheer"). The tie is ours.
- **Payoffs.** None. The text refuses a gradient (ch. ix, xiv); the game has no score and no best path.
- **Presentation.** The sickroom drawn as a cell (Given); the blood never red (Given); the people about the bed drawn once, at the laugh.

## 16. Mechanism kind and claim boundaries

**Toy.** An argument formalised. `mechanism.js` holds `CONFIG` (sixteen beats, the twenty of ch. xv, the turn probability, the floor on the first heaviness, the longest run, the hours), the weather `schedule(seed)` (drawn once from the seed, before the first look; no look is an argument to it), the three places with their queues (`CROSS` in the text's order, `WITHIN` in the text's order with the check of the cause first in any heaviness, `WINDOW` by the hour), the cheer (fires iff at the cross in heaviness with sureness next), the proffer (once, in the first heaviness after the drying), and `verdict()` in words. Every line the game says is in the file, tagged by chapter.

- **Demonstrates:** that a night can be played in which the feeling swings on its own clock, follows nothing the player does, and leaves the keeping untouched; that the sights can be reached in the text's order; that the change of cheer meets the one who stays.
- **Assumes:** the toy: the beats, the compression of twenty to about eight, the random schedule, the window and the hour, the horror at the edges, a sight read by the weather, the tie of the cheer and the proffer to beats, the blanket, the questions asked at the player's choosing, the sixteen-beat night ending wherever the schedule leaves it.
- **The text establishes:** an argument from a vision, not a finding: that the swing was not caused by her sin, that the keeping is constant, that the right act is to pass over. In the game these hold by construction; the game is a way of meeting them, not evidence for them, and About says so.

**Where the discovery comes from.** The independence of the weather from the looks is the text's claim (ch. xv) built in as a rule; it is not produced by a score, a budget or an objective, because there are none. Robustness (build.md §1): under four alternative settings of our own choices (turn probability 0.3 and 0.7, longest run 3, first heaviness by beat 2) the onset rate conditional on the last look stays equal across the three places within 0.07. A player who looks only at the window, only within, or flees to the window in every dark meets the same independence and the same blanket; what differs is what they saw.

## 17. Calibration plan and result

`node calibration.js`: 30 checks, all passed (`calibration-result.txt`).
