AIM: Force is yours, for now. Keep your soul through the war.   (life 1, learner)

— DAY 1 OF 12   you ■■■■■■   your pan UP ↑
  PICTURE: you (clay all through) | him: a boy, clay all through
  LINE: A boy, disarmed, clasps your knees. He offers a ransom for his life.
  SHERDS: STRIKE (kill him)  TAKE (take him alive)  PASS (as if he were not there)  RAISE (give him your hand)
  INTERVAL: the spear is still
  → RAISE.  GAME: You raised him up. He stands, a man again. It cost you none of your soul. It cost you prestige: men fear you a little less.
  HOMER/WEIL: “Moderation itself is not without its perils.”
  SCALES: Your pan sinks.
  NOW: you ■■■■■■   your pan UP ↑

— DAY 2 OF 12   you ■■■■■■   your pan UP ↑
  PICTURE: you (clay all through) | him: an armed man, stone to the eyes, armed
  LINE: An armed man, stone to the eyes. He will strike if you do not.
  SHERDS: STRIKE (kill him)  [cracked Take: He is armed.]  PASS (as if he were not there)  RAISE (give him your hand)
  INTERVAL: the spear is still
  → STRIKE.  GAME: You killed him.
  COST: Stone climbs to your knees.
  WHY: Force turns to stone the one who uses it, as surely as the one it is used on.
  HOMER/WEIL: “Somebody was here, and the next minute there is nobody here at all.”

END: You lived to the night of the old man's visit, stone to the hips.
  Of the men force put in your hands, you killed three and raised six up.
  Twice the scales turned, and a man stood over you.
  On the last night, you and the old man sat and looked at each other.
  Force turns whoever it touches into a thing: the one who uses it as surely as the one who suffers it. Nobody holds it for long. It is on loan, and the higher it lifts a man, the less he can stop his own arm, and the further he falls.
  “… learn that there is no refuge from fate, learn not to admire force, not to hate the enemy, nor to scorn the unfortunate.”
