c END: You climbed with him until the bargain, and refused everything from there. Your line follows the deed: the worse the thing, the firmer the no. Foot's line does not; it crosses at the brew and comes back at the cave.
   You and Foot agreed on four of seven; you parted at the brew, the beggar and the cave. You and the intention rule agreed on six.
   Mr Bad got you two times: the brew and the beggar. Each time you did what he asked where she would have refused.
   Philippa Foot, 1967: what you aim at "plays only a quite subsidiary role" in what we say about these cases. What matters is whether you would be harming someone or only failing to help him. Not harming is the stricter duty. So: harm against harm, do the least harm; help against help, help the most; harm against help, you may not harm one to help others. The brew is where her line parts from the intention rule; the cave is where it parts from it again, the other way.
