VERDICT: Every chain held.
  Thirteen cards across three jobs: three for three numbers multiplied, five for doubling, five for the square numbers. Each chain went on working when the Engine was handed numbers you had never seen.
  The cards carry the operations and the columns carry the numbers, and the two never mix: that is why one chain serves every n. A card that turns the prism back makes a cycle, and a cycle lets a handful of cards do the work of hundreds: three cards for three hundred and thirty in one of Lovelace’s examples, twenty-five for every Number of Bernoulli there is, however great n may be.
  The Engine originates nothing. It did what you knew how to order it to do, and nothing else.
