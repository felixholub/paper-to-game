# Julian's Window — note for the author

1. Look first at `screenshot-a-beat2-result.png` (the first heaviness: the page under dot-tone, scribbles at the edges, the blanket and the cross's own light spared, and her check of the cause: an empty hand), then `screenshot-a-beat7-result.png` (the drying at the cross in the dark, and the cheer changing with no time between: the room already in stars while the sight is still the dry face), `screenshot-a-beat9-selected.png` ("look up" offered from the window), and `screenshot-a-end.png` (the verdict in words, then the night as sixteen frames with the rose line under them). Then play it once on a phone, with your thumb: the stay-or-go decision in the dark is the game.
2. Everything tunable is in `CONFIG` at the top of `mechanism.js`, the only copy of the mechanism: the text's numbers (sixteen shewings, the twenty swings of ch. xv, the sureness first), each tagged with its chapter; and everything ours (the turn probability, the first heaviness forced by beat three, the longest run, the hours at the window, the ties of the cheer and the proffer). Every line the game says is in the same file (`CROSS`, `WITHIN`, `WINDOW`, `WEATHER`, `VERDICT`), tagged by chapter; the drawings are in `art.js`. Edit, run `node calibration.js` (30 checks: the schedule's shape over 400 seeds, independence of the weather from every way of playing and under four alternative settings, the sights' order, the cheer and the proffer, the verdict), then `python3 build.py`, which refuses to build if the embedded mechanism differs from the file. The text is typeset (CCEL's edition of Warrack), not scanned; chapter numbers are Warrack's.
3. What we were least sure about: (a) the weather as a seeded random process, where the text says only "freely" and "when He will", and its compression from about twenty swings in one shewing to about eight across a night; (b) the window, which the text does not have: it carries "Look up to Heaven" (ch. xix) and the hour, both ours; (c) a sight "dreadful" in heaviness and "sweet" in sureness, where ch. vii says one sight was "horrifying and dreadful, sweet and lovely" at once; (d) the change of cheer tied to being at the cross when the weather turns (ch. xxi says "willingly abiding in the same Cross ... unto the last point"; the tie to a beat is ours); (e) her words in the balloons are the text's, lightly shortened, which the skill's rule against quoting was bent for: a manga puts its text in balloons, and these lines are the text; (f) the drawings are ours, in a shōjo manner, not from any edition.

This game has had the builder's review only: screenshots and scripted browser sessions at 380 px. It has not been played by real strangers, and its length (about five minutes for a night) is an estimate.
