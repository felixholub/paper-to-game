          How to play

          Your aimUranium makes the air carry electricity. Find what else does.
          1. The bench. Whatever lies on the lower brass plate, if it sends out rays, lets the air between the two plates carry a tiny electric current. The current pushes the spot of light along the scale, to the right.
          2. Your thumb. The slider lets a weight down onto a quartz crystal. Squeezed by the weight, the quartz sends back the opposite electricity and pushes the spot to the left. The faster you let the weight down, the harder it pushes.
          3. Write it down. Press WRITE IT DOWN whenever you like. Your page keeps the push you had at that moment, as a plain number, and Marie Curie, just off the panel, says what your page now believes, right or wrong: in a speech balloon, a thought cloud, or a jagged shout when two readings clash. She remembers what your page said before.
          4. Your page. Each number is drawn as a panel. The yellow bar is what the uranium in it should give, the blue bar the number you wrote, the dashed line your own pure uranium. A few lines after the blue bar mean the spot was still running when you wrote it down. You have nine panels; the first is pure uranium.
          5. The shelf. Tap a jar or a rock to read its label: what it is, who sent it, how much uranium it holds. TEST IT puts it on the plate and spends a panel. You can test anything again; your page believes the latest number.
          6. The last pages. After nine panels your ideas are put together into a physics, three laws, and drawn as a world: what life would look like if your note were true. Then ours, and where the two part.
