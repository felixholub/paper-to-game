# Jevons's Noon: author note

**Look first at** the ruler (both rows: how big the fire gets, how long the coal lasts), then the 1885 engine card: an announcement, and the burn after it reports the year the works fitted the engine and the days fuel took. Then the ledger at the foot of the sheet filling generation by generation, with its box for after 2065, and the verdict's score and "Who got the coal". Then *The curve, continued*: the last screen has no words, by design (your Given).

**The config block** is at the top of `jevons/mechanism.js`. Every number is tagged with where it comes from: the frontispiece, the preface, ch. I, ch. VII, or "our choice". The production engine's anchors are `ALPHA` (0.7) and `FUEL_SHARE_1865` (a twentieth); the engines' terms are in `ENGINES` (`pays`, `cheapens`); the box after is `AFTER_YEARS`. The score's best path is found by `jevons/optimum.js` when `python3 jevons/build.py` builds the page (so a tweak to the mechanism moves the best path with it), and `calibration.js` checks the two agree. The words the game says are in `jevons/words.js`.

**Least sure about:** the score is ours three times over: the planner (log utility, no discounting, every generation the same), the heads (the people Britain would have had at the census rate, the gone counted in), and the box after (the coal left, burnt evenly over ten more generations). Each was chosen for a reason the memo gives (§22), and each moves the ranking: a planner who weighed the present more would rank "as now" above "slower". The production engine's form and anchors are ours too, and the far generations' per-head figures rest on a toy population two centuries long. `calibration.js` shows the directions and the score's order hold when every one of our choices is moved both ways. Also: the real series on the last screen is checked from 1900 on, but 1865–1899 is recalled from Mitchell, and Hull's 83,544 is from ch. II, which was not in the PDF.

The game has had the builder's review only (screenshots and a scripted browser session); no stranger has played it yet.
