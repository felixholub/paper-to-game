# Curie's Counterfactuals — note for the author

1. Revision 5 (2 Nov, midday): captions compare with the page (memo §26), the last page's three panels and paragraph are built from the page's own beliefs, a tap on a grey world name shows its picture and laws, a tap on any panel enlarges it.
2. Revision 4 (2 Nov, afternoon): ten worlds instead of five (memo §24), the game's own three closing panels after yours, directional speed lines and partings. `node world-tour.js` renders one designed page per world to `screenshot-world-<id>.png`; look at `doubt`, `bone` and `leaking` first. The dates in the closing panels of the restless atom and of ours are history and should be checked like the others; everything else is fiction.
3. Revision 3 (2 Nov) moved the world into play: after every panel the bench draws a dated scene of Paris, the shelf keeps them as a strip, and the last pages show the strip whole with the world a century on, beside ours. Look at `screenshot-c-bench-scene.png` and `screenshot-a-shelf-strip.png`, then the ends. The older notes follow.
4. Look first at the end of an album, which is where revision 2 lives: `screenshot-c-end.png` (the sky wind with living light: ideas pinned up, the physics card, the world of the note with its call-outs, ours, where they part), `screenshot-b-end.png` (the slow fire, from pure uranium written at half her push) and `screenshot-a-end.png` (her path measured carefully: "your page is her note"). On the bench, `screenshot-c-bench-lead.png` shows a balloon rising. Then play it carelessly once, on purpose, and write a rock down at its uranium: that is the bottomless atom.
5. Everything tunable is in `mechanism.js`, the only copy of the mechanism: her currents with their table rows in `CONFIG`; how a number is read against your own pure uranium (`BAND_*`); the four questions and their order of precedence in `settle()`, the ten worlds in `worldId()`; and every word the bench thinks and every world it draws in `WORDS` (balloons, clashes, the strip's scenes and the years ladder `YEARS`, laws, paragraphs, the scenes of ours, where they part). The drawings are in `template.html` (`SCENE`, `LANDMARK`). Edit, run `node calibration.js` (58 checks), then `python3 build.py`, which refuses to finish if the embedded mechanism differs from the file. The agreed design is `design-revision-2.md`.
6. What we were least sure about: (a) the worlds are our fiction, built to follow from their laws, and need your eye most: the slow fire's young Earth, the sky wind's mines and balloons, the crystal lens, the bottomless atom's free energy; (b) the dates on the page called Ours were written from memory and should be checked (polonium July 1898, radium December 1898, radium's heat 1903, the nucleus 1911, Hess 1912, fission 1938, element 93 in 1940, Patterson's 4.5 billion years in 1956, the ruby laser 1960, Kelvin's 20–40 million years); (c) the order of precedence among the wrong physics (a heavy metal beats a salt beats the copy) is a choice, and a page with several wrong numbers shows only the strongest as its title, the rest as secondary lines; (d) bands are read against the player's own uranium, so one bad yardstick reshapes the whole page, which is the point but can surprise; (e) the balloons speak for an unseen person at the bench, deliberately not Curie.

This game has had the builder's review only: screenshots and scripted browser sessions at 380 px. It has not been played by real strangers, and its length is an estimate.
