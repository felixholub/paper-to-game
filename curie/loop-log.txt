END: You found a rock stronger than pure uranium, and your copy of it was weak.
  You tested 8 things after pure uranium. Besides pure uranium, the spot moved for thin thorium oxide, white phosphorus, pitchblende from Saxony, chalcolite from a mine and thick thorium oxide.
  The black rock is only about two-thirds uranium, and it took three and a half times the push of pure uranium. Uranium cannot do that on its own.
  You made the same green crystal yourself, from pure chemicals: it took about a third of the push of pure uranium, like any uranium salt; the one from the mine took twice. So the extra is not the uranium and not the crystal: it is something in the rock that no bottle held.
  White phosphorus moved the spot; red phosphorus did not. Same element, another form, nothing: so it is not the phosphorus itself. The paper calls its action "probably of another nature".
  Thorium does it with no uranium at all, and your thick layer of it beat pure uranium.
  PAPER: Marie Curie tested dozens of metals, salts, oxides and rocks. Every uranium compound does it, about in proportion to its uranium. Thorium does it too. And two uranium rocks, pitchblende and chalcolite, do it far more than the uranium in them can. She made chalcolite herself from pure chemicals, and her copy was no stronger than any uranium salt.
  MEANS: So the rocks hold something no bottle in Paris held. Her words: these minerals "may contain an element much more active than uranium."
  LATER: After this note, and not in it: in July 1898 she and Pierre Curie announced one such element, traced in pitchblende, and named it polonium; in December, a second: radium.
