The world's rulebooks overlap: a bank's, a court's, an agency's. Where two of them say opposite things, they clash.

Nine times, you speak for an old international body (orange) whose rules clash with another's (green). You see how the clash began, choose how many pages of your rulebook to rewrite to meet them, and watch them answer.

Each game deals nine clashes from a pile of fourteen. Deal a different deck.

how to play

how to play

the picture

  • The orange circle is the body you speak for; the green one is theirs. Each has a rulebook; their rules are written under the picture.
  • Where the circles overlap, both rulebooks apply. In a clash they say opposite things there, and the overlap is striped: four orange stripes, four green.
  • Each clash begins one of two ways, and the picture shows which. A black dot falls into the overlap: a new problem nobody planned. Or a black arrow pushes their circle into yours: somebody started it on purpose. Sometimes both.

your move

  • Tap how many pages of your rulebook to rewrite to meet them, 0 to 4, then make the offer. Every offer starts at 0.
  • Each page turns one of your orange stripes green, and leaves your row of pages at the top. Pages you give are gone, deal or no deal.
  • If you gave enough, it's a deal: the stripes are overprinted olive and stitched. If not, you get a last word: one more offer, and what you already gave counts toward it. No deal after that is a stand-off.

the crowd

  • When a clash somebody started on purpose isn't settled by your first offer, black dots gather under one circle. That's the crowd: other governments, the press, people far away.
  • A crowd under their circle takes a page from you at once, and two more if the clash ends without a deal. A crowd under yours takes nothing.

years later

  • After each clash: what became of the two bodies. A deal usually knits them closer; a stand-off usually tears them apart. Not always, as in the paper's real cases.
  • Then a dashed box says what they would have settled for, and a grey line says which kind of clash the paper would call it.

keeping count

  • The squares at the top are the nine clashes: olive for a deal, crossed for a stand-off. The orange bars are your pages left.
