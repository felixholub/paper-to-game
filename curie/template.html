How to play

Your aimUranium makes the air carry electricity. Find what else does.
  1. The bench. Whatever lies on the lower brass plate, if it sends out rays, lets the air between the two plates carry a tiny electric current. The current pushes the spot of light along the scale, to the right.
  2. Your thumb. The slider lets a weight down onto a quartz crystal. Squeezed by the weight, the quartz pushes the spot back to the left. The faster you let the weight down, the harder it pushes.
  3. Balance it. Find the push that holds the spot still on 0. A blue ring closes around it; when it has closed, the measurement is done. How hard you had to push is how strong the plate is. If the spot never moves, the plate does nothing, and the ring closes by itself.
  4. Your page. Each measurement is drawn as a panel on your album page. The yellow bar is what the thing's uranium alone could give; the blue bar is what it gave; the dashed line is pure uranium. You have nine panels. The first is pure uranium: your yardstick.
  5. The shelf. Tap a jar or a rock to read its label: what it is, who sent it, how much uranium it holds. TEST IT puts it on the plate and spends a panel. Each thing can be tested once.
  6. The last page. After nine panels: what you found, and what Marie Curie found in 1898.
