An Engine of figure-wheels that can add, subtract, multiply and divide, and does nothing at all until cards are hung over its prism. Then it does exactly what the cards say.

You will punch cards, hang them in a chain, set the Engine going, and listen for the bell. Then the Engine is handed numbers you have not seen, and runs your chain again.

The Countess's own chain

Note G ends with the Numbers of Bernoulli: a chain with a cycle inside a cycle. Here it is, run on this Engine, each Number worked from the ones before it. You hang nothing; you watch.

Were the fundamental relations of pitched sounds expressible in this way, she wrote, the Engine might compose elaborate and scientific pieces of music of any degree of complexity or extent.

The Engine

The columns of figure-wheels are the Store. Each column holds one number and has a name in the square beneath it (a, n, x; or R for a blank result column; or 1 for a column that simply holds one). The numbers you are given are already on the wheels.

The paper strip beside the Store shows what is wanted, faint. What the Engine actually prints appears next to it in ink.

Cards

A card tells the Engine one thing. To punch one, tap a punch on the blank card, then tap columns in the Store:

  • + − × ÷ take the first column you tap, combine it with the second, and put the result on the third. a × n → R means: multiply a by n and put the result on R. A column may be used twice: R × x → R.
  • PRINT prints a column on the strip.
  • BACK turns the prism back a number of cards, so those cards do their work again, while the column you tap is not nothing. When that column reaches 0 the prism goes on past. BACK 2 WHILE n ≠ 0 repeats the two cards above it until n is 0.

Tap Hang the card to add it to the chain. Tap a hung card to take it down. The rack holds ten cards.

Setting it going

The Engine presents your cards to the prism one after another. Wheels turn, the strip prints. If the strip matches what was wanted, the bell rings. Then the Engine is handed other numbers and runs your chain again, untouched. A chain that rings the bell every time has held.

If the bell does not ring, the line under the Engine says what it printed instead. Change the chain and set it going again. After two runs you may hand the task to the Countess and see her way.

Three tasks

a·n·x; then a·xⁿ, where the Engine will be handed other n; then the table of squares, with additions and subtractions only, as far as n. All three are examples from the Notes.
