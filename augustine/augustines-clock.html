How to play

Measure how long things last, with no clock but your memory.

The circle is now. In the middle of the screen is a black circle on a black line. It is the present moment. Above the line is what is still to come, drawn dashed. Below the line is memory: what is over, drawn solid.

Nothing is drawn while it lasts. While a voice sounds, the circle turns red, and that is all. When the voice stops, a red bar drops into memory. While you hold, the circle turns blue. When you let go, a blue bar drops in under the red one, so you can see which was longer.

Four voices. The low one, the middle one, the high one and the top one, each its own length. You hear a voice by touching the circle. You give it back by pressing and holding the circle as long as it lasted. The game says how close you came: the same, nine tenths, half, double.

Asked for again. The game asks for a voice you heard earlier: with nothing sounding, through static, or with a different voice in your ears. Hold the one you are asked for.

The silence. After the high one, a hum starts and a wheel turns. Pressing the circle cuts the hum: your silence. Hold the silence as long as the high one lasted. The wheel is out there, and the potter works it unevenly.

The tumult. You hear the low one once more, alone. Then press and hold: the crowd starts when you press, several voices at once under the sun, the low one among them. Let go the moment the low one stops; the others go on. Afterwards, say which voices were in the crowd.

The last silence. Nothing sounds. Tap once to begin, say the low one in your head, and tap again when it would have ended.

The last page says what you did, what Augustine says about it, and shows the low one each time you held it across the evening.

About

The game in one line

Four voices, each heard once, and then asked for again under worse and worse conditions: with nothing sounding, through static, with a different voice in your ears, in a silence with a potter's wheel turning in it, inside a crowd under a sun that stops, and at the end in silence, said in your head. You measure with nothing but what stayed in you. Nothing is drawn while it lasts; what is over drops into memory.

What the book says

In Book XI of the Confessions Augustine asks what time is. "If no one asks me, I know: if I wish to explain it to one that asketh, I know not." The past is no longer, the future is not yet, and the present has no length. Yet we measure times and compare them, and say this one was double that. A learned man told him that time is the motion of the sun, moon and stars. But a potter's wheel that ran now slower, now quicker would have rounds of different lengths, and we would know it; and when the sun once stood still, "time went on". Motions are measured by time, so they are not time. A voice cannot be measured before it sounds, because it is not yet; nor while it sounds, because it has not ended; nor after, because it is gone. "It is not then themselves, which now are not, that I measure, but something in my memory, which there remains fixed. It is in thee, my mind, that I measure times." We measure silence by stretching our thought to a voice "as if it sounded". A psalm said by heart drains from expectation, through attention, into memory; so does a life. And a mind stretched among many things, he says in the next chapter, is also torn among them. Book X supplies the store: the "fields and spacious palaces" of memory, where images stay when the things are gone and older ones "glide back, as it were, into the deeper recesses".

How the game plays it

The voices are ours: four pitches, each dealt a length between two and four seconds, at least half a second apart, with the top one clearly unlike the low one. Each station is one of the book's measurements. A voice heard and held (ch. XXVII). The low one asked for again with nothing sounding, and the middle one through static, which is attention tax and tells you nothing about how long (X.viii; XI.xxviii). The low one held while the top one sounds, the voice in your ears against the voice in your memory (XI.xxviii). A silence held as long as the high one, with a hum that pressing cuts and a wheel turning out there, which the potter works unevenly through the silence, now slower, now quicker (XI.xxiii, xxvii). A crowd of voices that starts when you press, under a sun that climbs and stands still partway, with the low one to be held until it drops out and the others going on, which is the battle the sun stood still for (XI.xxiii, xxix); then, which voices were in it, one of them a stranger. And last, the low one said in your head, with nothing sounding and nothing held, the silence measured "to the measure of a voice, as if it sounded" (XI.xxvii).

What the words mean

After every measure the game says in words where your hold landed against its target: the same, nine tenths, half, double. No decimal ever reaches the screen. Two readings are ours and are stated only as where the hold landed, never as what you were thinking: a hold in the silence is read as "where counting the wheel would put you" when it lies nearer, in proportion, to the moment the wheel's third turn was done than to the voice (the paces are dealt so that the two are well apart); a hold against the top one is read as pulled when it lies nearer the top one's length than the low one's. In the tumult, holding until the crowd ends is read as waiting for the sun. The last page composes a headline from these, shows the low one each time you held it across the evening, and ends with chapter XXIX.

What the book establishes

An argument, not a measurement: that the present has no length, that past and future are in the mind as memory and expectation, that time is not the motion of any body, and that we measure times in the mind. How close your holds came is no evidence for any of this, or against it. Augustine starts from the fact that we do measure; the game lets you do it, and lets everything he names pull at it. The referee of your holds is the phone's clock, which Augustine would call one more motion measured by time; the game uses it and nothing on screen calls it time.

What is left out

The opening prayers and the account of creation by the Word (XI.i–ix). The question what God was doing before he made heaven and earth (XI.x–xiii, xxx): one line on the last page. God's knowledge, which has no expectation and no memory (XI.xxxi). Foretelling the sunrise from the daybreak (XI.xviii). The hymn "Deus Creator omnium" and its syllables (XI.xxvii), which an earlier build played and this one does not. From Book X, everything but memory's store. And all of Augustine's life.
