AIM: Uranium makes the air carry electricity. Find what else does.

PANEL 1 · PURE URANIUM (careful) → YARD
  GAME: Pure uranium, your yardstick: you pushed about as hard as she measured.
  💭 So this is pure uranium: the air between the plates carries this much electricity. Everything else gets measured against it.

PANEL 2 · LEAD (written at 0.5) → ACTIVE, spot still running left
  GAME: Lead: the air carried about half as much electricity as with your pure uranium. Hers: none.
  💭 Wow! The air carries electricity near lead! Every heavy atom does it, the heavier the more… as if they caught something raining through space.

PANEL 3 · THIN THORIUM OXIDE (careful) → ACTIVE
  GAME: Thin thorium oxide: the air carried about as much electricity as with your pure uranium. Hers: about as much electricity as with her pure uranium.
  💭 Thorium does it, with no uranium in it! Not one strange metal: the two heaviest atoms there are. Hmm… does the table of elements end where atoms start to leak?

PANEL 4 · WHITE PHOSPHORUS (careful) → STRONG
  GAME: White phosphorus: the air carried a little more electricity than with your pure uranium. Hers: a little more electricity than with her pure uranium.
  💭 White phosphorus, stronger than uranium! And phosphorus is in every bone… is the stuff of life electric?

PANEL 5 · PITCHBLENDE FROM SAXONY (careful) → HIGH
  GAME: Pitchblende from Saxony: the air carried three and a half times as much electricity as with your pure uranium. Hers: three and a half times as much electricity as with her pure uranium.
  💭 More than its uranium can give?! Something heavier than uranium is hiding in that rock: a bigger sail!

PANEL 6 · CHALCOLITE FROM A MINE (careful) → HIGH, spot still running right
  GAME: Chalcolite from a mine: the air carried twice as much electricity as with your pure uranium. Hers: twice as much electricity as with her pure uranium.
  💭 More than its uranium! Something in the rock… or the way the crystal is built?

PANEL 7 · CHALCOLITE MADE BY YOU (careful) → ABOUT
  GAME: Chalcolite made by you: the air carried about a third as much electricity as with your pure uranium. Hers: about a third as much electricity as with her pure uranium.
  💭 My own chalcolite is ordinary. So it isn’t the crystal: the mine’s crystal holds something mine doesn’t!

PANEL 8 · LEAD (careful) → NONE
  GAME: Lead: the air carried no electricity you could see. Hers: none.
  💭 Second look: lead: heavy, and nothing. So it isn’t weight alone.

PANEL 9 · RED PHOSPHORUS (careful) → NONE
  GAME: Red phosphorus: the air carried no electricity you could see. Hers: none.
  💭 Red phosphorus: nothing. Same element as white phosphorus, another form: so it was never the phosphorus. The white kind was just burning slowly in the air.

YOUR PHYSICS: THE RESTLESS ATOM
  1. The energy comes from inside the heaviest atoms, and an atom that spends it turns into another element.  (panels 5, 6)
  2. Only the heaviest atoms do it, uranium and thorium, the two heaviest known: the table of elements ends where atoms start to leak.  (panels 3)
  3. Old uranium rocks are full of what uranium turned into: new elements, far stronger than uranium.  (panels 5, 6, 7)

YOUR WORLD (if it were true): landmark dome; vignettes rockclock, hospital, table118
  (1) Atoms of uranium split in power stations: a million times the energy of any fire.  ← panels 5, 6
  (2) A rock cut open is a clock: the atoms that changed in it say the Earth is four and a half billion years old.
  (3) Rays from changing atoms treat cancer.
  (4) The table of elements ends where atoms fall apart: 118, the last ones made by hand.
  In this world, as in ours, two new elements came out of tons of pitchblende within the year: polonium and radium (1898). Radium keeps itself warmer than the room (1903); its rays, fired at gold leaf, showed that the atom has a tiny, heavy heart (1911); atoms of uranium were split (1938), first for a weapon, then for power. Rocks are dated by the atoms that changed in them: the Earth is four and a half billion years old.
