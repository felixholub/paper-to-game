The game in one line

Ten measurements made with nothing but your mind, each one a step in Augustine's argument about where a length of time is held: a moment of your own life that no clock ever measured; a voice held after it is gone; last night against yesterday, which only a clock measured, as a relation of two sounds; the half of a voice while it sounds; a silence you plan and then recognise among voices; the sun's standing still, measured by a sound; a hymn sung back and its longs measured by its shorts; a crowd; and at the end your night and day and your moment of joy, recognised again or not. Nothing is drawn while it lasts; what is over drops into memory.

What the book says

In Book XI of the Confessions Augustine asks what time is. "If no one asks me, I know: if I wish to explain it to one that asketh, I know not." The past is no longer, the future is not yet, and the present has no length. Yet we measure times and compare them, and say this one was double that. Where, then, is a length held? He answers by elimination. Not in the thing: a voice cannot be measured before it sounds, nor while it sounds, nor after, when it is gone. Not in the motion of bodies: a learned man told him time is the motion of the sun, moon and stars, but a potter's wheel running now slower, now quicker has rounds of different lengths, and when the sun once stood still over a battle "time went on"; we measure a body's motion, and its standing still, by time, so time is not its motion. Not in the future or the past as such, which are not. "It is not then themselves, which now are not, that I measure, but something in my memory, which there remains fixed. It is in thee, my mind, that I measure times." The man who plans a long sound "hath in silence already gone through a space of time". We measure silence by stretching our thought to a voice "as if it sounded". A psalm said by heart drains from expectation, through attention, into memory; so does a life. Time is a stretching of the mind (distentio), and in chapter XXIX the same word turns: a mind stretched among many things is torn among them, and he asks to be gathered. Book X supplies the store: the "fields and spacious palaces" of memory, where images stay when the things are gone.

How the game plays it

Every station is one of the book's measurements, done by the player. A moment of joy held for as long as it lasted: a span nothing outside the player ever measured (X.viii). The low one heard and held: the measure is in you, since the voice is gone (XI.xxvii). Last night and yesterday as two sounds in a relation: the night is the one span of a life not attended to at all, known only by a clock outside; what can be given is the relation, not the lengths (XI.xx). The low one let go at its half while it sounds: nothing can be measured in the present; what passes is laid against what is expected, and the expectation comes from memory (XI.xxi, xxviii). A silence held with nothing sounding, then two voices, one of them that long: the planned span had gone through a space of time without sounding (XI.xxvii). The sun crossing a sky of a different width at a different pace each evening, standing still on the way, and its standing still measured by a sound: a body's rest is measured by time, so time is not the body's motion (XI.xxiii–xxiv). The hymn's line sung back, the longs measured by the shorts, and the player asked which of their own syllables was longest: every comparison is between two things in memory (XI.xxvii). A crowd of voices, the low one to be held until it drops out, then how many there were: a mind stretched among several things holds each less (XI.xxix). And the two inner spans offered back among decoys, at the end: the only measures checked against the player rather than the world.

What the words mean

After a hold the game says in words where it landed against its target: the same, nine tenths, half, double. No decimal ever reaches the screen. Where there is no target (the moment, the night and day, the planned silence) the game says so, and later offers the player's own span back among decoys half again as long or two thirds of it, or pairs at three sizes with the player's relation in one; a right pick is a right pick and nothing more is read into it. In the crowd, a release within a few tenths of the low one's end is "on the moment"; holding until every voice has stopped is "to the end". The headline on the last page counts the three inner spans known again; the pictures show them beside what was offered, and the world-checked measures with the thing above and yours below.

What the book establishes

An argument, not a measurement: that the present has no length, that past and future are in the mind as memory and expectation, that time is not the motion of any body, and that we measure times in the mind, as a stretching that is also a scattering. How close a player's holds come is no evidence for any of this, or against it. Augustine starts from the fact that we do measure; the game lets you do it in each of the places he looks, and lets you feel where the measure was. The referee of the world-checked holds is the phone's clock, which Augustine would call one more motion measured by time; the game uses it and nothing on screen calls it time. The inner spans have no referee at all, which is the point.

What is left out

The opening prayers and the account of creation by the Word (XI.i–ix). The question what God was doing before he made heaven and earth (XI.x–xiii, xxx): one line on the last page. God's knowledge, which has no expectation and no memory (XI.xxxi). Foretelling the sunrise from the daybreak (XI.xviii). The potter's wheel (XI.xxiii), which earlier builds played and this one leaves to the sun. The half of a voice never heard before, which has no right answer: a paragraph on the last page. From Book X, everything but memory's store. And all of Augustine's life.

The text
