a END (No time between): Night 7. The night ended early.
  You did not see the day. Before dawn, after the eighth beat, you were taken from all your pain and from all your woe, with no time between. It came after you had looked at the cross, as it would have come after anything.
  The heaviness lay on the room three times. In it you stayed at the cross twice, went to the window not once, and looked within once. At the cross you saw the garland, the drops, the face, the scourging, the laugh, the drying and the question; you did not reach the side, His mother and "It is I". Within, you asked what brought the heaviness. The cheer changed for you once, with no time between.
  The heaviness came three times, each time after the cross; after one thing, as it would have come after any other. She checked the same: "I sinned not wherefore I should be left to myself; also I deserved not to have this blessed feeling." The blanket never changed: "He keepeth us even alike secure in woe and in weal." And: "it is not God’s will that we follow the feelings of pain in sorrow and mourning for them, but that we suddenly pass over." She was not taken that night; she lived, and marvelled at it, and wrote the taking down as she had been shown it: "we shall suddenly be taken from all our pain and from all our woe, and of His Goodness we shall come up above."
  The dark that comes over you is not a message about you. Between the dark and the light there is no time. What you saw, you saw by staying; what you sought and did not see counted the same. The heaviness had no climb: the last was no worse than the first, and it led nowhere. That is the point, not a fault of the night. What climbed was the sight at the cross, from the garland to the drying, and only past the drying does the cheer change. The taking came as the heaviness came: after nothing you did, with no time between. Whether you saw the day was never in your hands, any more than the weather was; what was in your hands was where you looked, and what you saw.
