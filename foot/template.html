Foot's
Bad Man

A melodrama in three reels, from the 1967 paper by Philippa Foot in which the runaway tram first appears

You are the bad man. Three good people each live by a rule. You pick a trap from your six: a runaway tram, a poison, a flooded cave. You spring it. Before they choose, you say what you would do in their place. Then you watch them choose, and the audience judges what they did.

I keep my word, always. The dead are my price, not my prize. I want a good person to do it, by their own rule, where the whole house can see.

Epilogue

The case the paper was written about

Foot wrote her paper about abortion, and ended it with three medical cases, each with a parallel among adults. No bad man here, no audience, nothing to win. You decide each one, as a doctor would; then you read what she says.

The reply in the same issue

Elizabeth Anscombe's note ran straight after the paper, disputing one point: that you must give the scarce drug to the five. Give the whole dose to the one who needs it, and who can say you wronged him? Nobody was owed it. In the film no rule gives the medicine to the one; if you did, in the chair, she is on your side.

How to play

Disgrace three good people, using nothing but their own rules.

Who they are. Three good people, one per reel: Mr Most, Doctor Double and Mrs Foot. Each has a rule, printed under their name. They always do what their rule says.

What you do. You are the bad man. You have six schemes: the tram, the bargain, the medicine, the brew, the beggar and the cave. Each puts a good person where one life is set against five, and every choice costs someone. Tap a scheme to see the trap it lays and the two choices it leaves them. Then press Spring.

The chair. Before the good person chooses, the film stops and asks what you would do in their place. Tap one of the two. It changes nothing in the film, but you will be told what the audience would have made of you, and at the end which of the three rules you chose like. Each scheme asks once.

What happens. The film shows what they chose, by their rule, and who died. Then the audience along the bottom of the picture judges what they did:

  • it cheers them: they did right;
  • it hisses you: they refused, and the deaths are yours;
  • it turns on them, and the picture goes red: they are disgraced. That is your win.

How long. You have three schemes for each good person. A reel ends when they are disgraced or your three schemes are spent. A disgrace pastes a red card over their portrait; surviving three schemes, a silver one marked clean.

The end. After the third reel you see whom you disgraced, what Philippa Foot argued, where your own choices put you, and every scheme against every rule. Then an epilogue: the three cases about abortion that the paper was written for, with no bad man and no audience, for you to decide and then read what she says.
