| **Goodall's Long Grass** | van Lawick-Goodall (1970), *Advances in the Study of Behavior* 3:195–249. Tool-using in primates and other vertebrates, as a field notebook in graphite: you are a young chimpanzee at Gombe. Pick three stems in the long grass, out of sight of the mound; push one down a termite hole, wait, draw it out without shaking them off, while Goodall's red pencil says which age of infant fishes the way you just did (too thick, too floppy, a jerk: two to three; five to eight inches, a pause, a smooth pull: grown-up). Then water in a hollow tree, and the same push and pull. | [play](https://steffenhuck.github.io/paper-to-game/goodall/goodalls-long-grass.html) · [memo](goodall/memo.md) |
