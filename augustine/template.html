AUGUSTINE'SCLOCK

What then is time? If no one asks me, I know: if I wish to explain it to one that asketh, I know not.Augustine, Confessions XI

Ten measurements, made with nothing but your mind: a moment of your own life, a voice after it has stopped, last night against yesterday, the half of a voice while it sounds, a silence you plan, the sun standing still, a hymn, a crowd. Each time you press and hold a black circle, or say which of some voices was yours. There is no clock.

Where is a length of time held? Find out by measuring, with no clock but your mind.

How to play

Where is a length of time held? Measure, with no clock but your mind.

The circle is now. In the middle of the screen is a black circle on a black line. It is the present moment. Above the line is what is still to come, drawn dashed. Below the line is memory: what is over, drawn solid.

Nothing is drawn while it lasts. While a voice sounds, the circle turns red, and that is all. When the voice stops, a red bar drops into memory. While you hold, the circle turns blue. When you let go, a blue bar drops in, so you can see which was longer.

Holding. Most stations ask you to press and hold the circle for as long as something lasted: a moment of your own life, a voice you just heard, the sun standing still. The game then says in words how close you came: the same, nine tenths, half, double. Where there is nothing to check against, it says so.

Choosing. Some stations end with a question in big blue letters and a row of buttons under it: which of two or three voices was yours, which of your syllables was longest, how many voices were in the crowd. Touch one.

Night and day. Two holds in a row, a low note for last night and a high note for yesterday, so that the two sounds stand to each other as the night stood to the day. Only the relation is kept.

Half of it. Hear the low one once more. Then press and hold: it sounds again while you hold. Let go at its half. It goes on to its end, so you hear how much was still to come.

The hymn. Hear a line of eight syllables, short and long. The line stays in memory as words only. Sing it back with eight holds, each long one twice a short one; your line grows in memory as you go. Then say which of your eight was the longest.

The tumult. Press and hold, and a crowd of voices starts. Let go the moment the low one stops; the others go on. Then say how many voices there were.

The last page says what you did, what Augustine says about it, and shows the three spans that were only ever in you beside what was offered for them.
