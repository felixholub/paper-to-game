# Augustine's Clock — note for the author

1. Look first at `screenshot-a-r3-kicked.png` and `screenshot-a-r3-result.png` (the wheel kicked while you hold; the player who counted its ticks lands at half the voice, and the game asks how you knew it was racing), then `screenshot-a-r6-result.png` (a short syllable laid along a long one that never sounded with it) and `screenshot-a-end.png`. Then play it once on a phone with the sound on: the game is the hold, and the wheel.
2. Everything tunable is in `CONFIG` at the top of `mechanism.js`, the only copy of the mechanism: the book's numbers (the hymn line, its four short and four long, the long exactly double the short, the chain of ch. XV, each tagged with chapter and page), and everything ours (voice lengths, three turns per voice, the kick and when it lands, the sun's arc, the hold cap, the hymn's tempo and melody, the reading of the sun round). The words the game says are in the same file (`hold`, `silence`, `sing`, `lay`, `verdict`, `AMOUNTS`). Edit, run `node calibration.js` (31 checks: the book's numbers, the draw, the words, six simulated players over whole evenings, eight variants of our choices), then `python3 build.py`, which refuses to build if the embedded mechanism differs from the file. The text is typeset (CCEL), so no number is read off a scan.
3. What we were least sure about: (a) the kick, which is ours: the book only says a wheel *could* run faster or slower, and the game makes it happen at the press so the counting strategy fails by a visible amount; (b) scoring a measure at all, since Augustine scores nobody. The fraction words are a yardstick for the player, and the last page compares them with nobody; (c) the readings "just where counting the wheel would put you" and "you waited for the sun", which a shaky timer can earn without counting (about one evening in six); (d) the phone's clock as referee, an irony About owns; (e) the melody, which is ours, not the Ambrosian chant; (f) "no biography" was read strictly: Augustine appears only as the author of the sentences.

This game has had the builder's review only: screenshots and scripted browser sessions at 380 px. It has not been played by real strangers, and its length (about five to six minutes) is an estimate.
